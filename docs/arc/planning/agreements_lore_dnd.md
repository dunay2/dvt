# The Oracle Dilemma — Agreements as D&D Lore

Date: 2026-02-25

## The Tower of Divination

In the citadel of DVT+, the Apprentice climbed the spiral stairs to the Chamber of Glass, where the Oracle watched the threads of fate.

> “Archmage… the Oracle cannot see the future. There is no history to consult.”

The Archmage did not flinch.

> “Then the Oracle is not broken. Only **starved**. And an Oracle must never be silent.”

Thus, the First Law was inscribed into the stone:

### The First Law: The Oracle Never Goes Quiet

Even when the pages of history are blank, the Oracle speaks—clearly marking what it knows and what it guesses.

The Apprentice hesitated.

> “But if it guesses… will we lie to the traveler?”

The Archmage raised a finger.

> “Never. The Oracle must always reveal the **source** of its vision and the **confidence** of its omen.”

So the Oracle’s tongue was bound to truth:

- Portent when it has seen battles firsthand,
- Eco when it listens to distant wars,
- Structural when it reads the geometry of old battle-plans,
- Augury when it relies on doctrine and heuristics,
- Simulation when it invokes the dice of probability.

## The Five Spells of the Oracle

### 1) Portent

When enough battles have been recorded in the same realm, the Oracle’s prophecy is strong and precise.

### 2) Eco

When the realm is young and lacks scars, the Oracle listens to the echoes of other kingdoms—
but only when many voices speak at once, so that no single household can be identified.
(k-anonymity wards were etched into the stones.)

### 3) Structural Sight

If no battle was fought, but the generals drew plans, the Oracle studies the plans’ complexity:
dependencies, depth, and branching.

### 4) Augury

If plans are absent, the Oracle follows doctrine:
models are heavier than tests; deeper webs cost more than straight roads.

### 5) The Monte Carlo Dice

When nothing else exists, the Oracle casts a thousand dice,
not to pretend certainty, but to avoid silence.

## The Two Great Maps

The citadel contained two maps, never to be confused:

1. The Map of Oaths (Governance)
   A ledger of scrolls, laws, and binding decisions—
   Files and Modules tied to ADRs by sacred headers.

2. The Map of Roads (Lineage)
   The living routes of caravans—
   Jobs, Datasets, and Runs traced by the standard of the guild.

The Archmage declared:

> “We do not merge these maps prematurely.
> We link them by stable names, and each remains sovereign.”

## The Guild Standard: OpenLineage & the Hall of Marquez

To avoid forging a new dialect, the citadel adopted a guild standard:

- OpenLineage as the lingua franca of roads and journeys,
- Marquez as the hall where travelers may inspect paths and past runs.

And so the citadel’s modules were judged:

- The Planner, Verifier, Interpreter, Engine, and State Store remained the heart of the realm.
- The Traceability Service remained the keeper of oaths—never replaced by the guild’s road maps.
- New emissaries were commissioned:
  - the OpenLineage Emitter to speak in the guild tongue,
  - the Marquez Publisher to send messages to the hall.

## Final Inscription

The Apprentice bowed.

> “So even without history, we do not ship silence.”

The Archmage smiled once.

> “Correct. We ship truthful prophecy—marked, bounded, and improving with every run.”
